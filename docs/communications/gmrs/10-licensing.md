# GMRS Licensing

!!! note "As of September 25, 2026"
    The regulatory details on this page — fees, license term, eligibility and
    operating rules — are as of **September 25, 2026**. FCC fees and Part 95
    rules do change; confirm current requirements in ULS and the eCFR before
    you apply.

## Summary

Operating GMRS equipment requires an individual FCC license — with one
exception: anyone may transmit a GMRS emergency message without a license.
The license is cheap, exam-free, and broad: one $35 application covers every
GMRS station you own (handhelds, vehicle radios, a base at home, repeaters)
anywhere in the United States and its territories, for ten years.

## Who can hold a license

- An **individual who is at least 18 years old** is eligible to obtain a new
  individual GMRS license ([§95.1705(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-95.1705)).
  There is no exam, no technical test, and no citizenship requirement.
- **No new corporate or business licenses are issued.** The only non-individual
  GMRS licenses that exist are a small number of grandfathered licenses held
  by entities that renewed licenses granted before July 31, 1987
  ([§95.1701](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-95.1701)). If your
  business needs multiple operators, each operator should hold their own
  individual license — business communications are a permitted use of the
  service, but the list of who may *operate* a station is limited (below).
- A person under 18 **cannot be a licensee** but may operate stations under an
  adult's license; the licensee is solely responsible for the minor's
  operation ([§95.1743](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-95.1743)).

## Who may operate your stations

Under your individual license, you may allow:

1. **Yourself**, obviously.
2. **Immediate family members** — spouse, children, grandchildren,
   stepchildren, parents, grandparents, stepparents, brothers, sisters, aunts,
   uncles, nieces, nephews, and in-laws ([§95.1705(d)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-95.1705)).
3. **Anyone**, if necessary to communicate an emergency message
   ([§95.1705(d)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-95.1705)).

Note that employees, neighbors, and friends are *not* on the list. For a
work crew or club where several people need to talk, the practical answers are
one license per person (they're cheap), FRS radios (unlicensed, but no
repeaters and lower power — see
[Frequencies and Power](20-frequencies-and-power.md)), or ham radio for the
operators willing to take the exam.

## How to apply

The application is done online in the FCC's **Universal Licensing System
(ULS)** (<https://www.fcc.gov/uls>); there is no paper form.

1. Create a ULS account (a free "FCC Registration" / CORES account if you
   don't have one).
2. Start a new application and select the **General Mobile Radio Service**
   license type.
3. Enter your name, mailing address, email, and phone number. That's the
   substance of it — GMRS is an individual (personal) license with no
   technical parameters to file.
4. Pay the application fee: **$35** per the personal-license fee schedule in
   [47 CFR 1.1102(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-1/subpart-G).
   The fee schedule also notes an associated regulatory fee for GMRS; ULS
   will show the exact total at checkout.

Processing is quick by FCC standards (days, not weeks). When granted, you
receive an **FCC call sign** (e.g., `KCxxxx`); it appears in your ULS record.
No paper license card arrives and none is required; the ULS database is the
system of record. Keep a screenshot or printout in each vehicle that carries a
radio.

## Your responsibilities as licensee

- **Station identification.** Each station must identify with your call sign
  at the end of each exchange and at least every 15 minutes during continuous
  operation — see [Operating Procedure](50-operations.md#station-identification).
- **Supervision.** You are responsible for improper operation by anyone using
  stations under your license, including family members and minors.
- **Inspection and records.** On request by an FCC representative you must
  make the station available for inspection and keep basic station records
  (responses to FCC notices, written permissions, sharing agreements)
  ([§95.1723](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-95.1723)). In
  practice this bites almost nobody who operates sensibly.
- **Compliance.** You are responsible for keeping your equipment within the
  service's technical limits — in practice, buy certified equipment and don't
  modify it (see [Radio Selection](30-radio-selection.md)).

## Renewal and changes

- An individual license is granted for a **term of ten years** from the date
  of grant or renewal ([§95.1705(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-95.1705)).
  The FCC may shorten a term at renewal as a sanction for rule violations.
- **Renew before the expiration date** shown in ULS; there is no automatic
  grace period in the GMRS rules, and operating on an expired license is not
  authorized. Renewal is another $35 application in ULS under the same
  license.
- Name or address changes are handled as a **modification** of the existing
  license, also via ULS ($35).

!!! tip "Household practice"
    One license per household is the norm: the head of household applies, and
    every family member listed above operates legally under it. Keep the call
    sign written on or next to each radio — it's what you say when identifying.
