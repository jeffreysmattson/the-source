# Ham Radio Licensing

!!! note "As of September 25, 2026"
    The regulatory details on this page — exam structure, fees, license term,
    and operating privileges — are as of **September 25, 2026**. FCC Part 97
    rules change periodically; confirm current requirements in the eCFR before
    you test.

## Summary

Operating amateur radio equipment requires an FCC-issued operator license.
There is no exam for GMRS or FRS, but ham radio has three tiers — Technician,
General, and Amateur Extra — each a multiple-choice test drawn from a public
question pool. The Technician class alone covers all the frequencies you need
for local voice communication (2 m, 70 cm, and above) plus limited HF access.
The license is non-renewable: it is granted for the life of the holder, with a
ten-year renewal window to confirm you are still operating or still alive.

## License classes

| Class | Exam | Privileges (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| **Technician** | 35 questions, 70% to pass | Full privileges on VHF/UHF/microwave (144–148 MHz, 222–225 MHz, 420–450 MHz, and above); limited HF: 80 m (3.5–3.6 MHz), 40 m (7.0–7.3 MHz, CW/data only below 7.175), 20 m (14.0–14.35 MHz, data only), 15 m (21.0–21.45 MHz, data only), 10 m (28.0–29.7 MHz) |
| **General** | Technician + 35 additional questions, 70% to pass | All Technician privileges plus full voice/data on most HF bands: 80 m, 40 m, 30 m, 20 m, 17 m, 15 m, 12 m, 10 m (all sub-bands above 3.5 MHz except the CW-only segments) |
| **Amateur Extra** | General + 50 additional questions, 75% to pass | All frequencies allocated to the Amateur Radio Service, including the CW/data-only "phone" exceptions on 80/40 m and exclusive access to certain sub-bands |

The question pools are public. The current pools, sample exams, and study
resources are at the [ARRL eLicense](https://elicense.arrl.org/) site and in
the ARRL's *Ham Radio License Manual*. You can also use third-party apps (W5YI,
Ham Radio License Exam, etc.) that draw from the same pool.

## Who may hold a license

- **Any person age 14 or older** may be licensed. A person under 14 cannot be
  licensed but may operate under the supervision of a licensed operator
  ([§97.501](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.501)).
- **No citizenship or residency requirement.** Non-US persons may be licensed;
  the license is valid for operation in the United States and its territories.
  Foreign amateurs operating temporarily in the US under a reciprocal agreement
  are governed by §97.511.
- There is no technical prerequisite, no age maximum, and no physical
  requirement. The exam is entirely multiple-choice on radio theory,
  regulations, and operating practice.

## How to test

1. **Find a Volunteer Examiner (VE) session.** Sessions are run by teams of
   three or more licensed amateurs (one must be Extra class). In New England,
   the ARRL New England Division and local amateur radio clubs schedule
   sessions roughly monthly; check the [ARRL VE search](https://www.arrl.org/find-a-ve-session)
   or your local club's calendar.
2. **Bring identification.** A government-issued photo ID (driver's license,
   passport, state ID). The VE team will verify your identity and record your
   SSN for the FCC application.
3. **Take the exam(s).** Each class is a separate sitting of 35 questions
   (Extra is 50). You may take multiple classes in one session — e.g., if you
   have no license, you can sit for Technician and General back-to-back.
4. **Wait for the call sign.** The VE team files your application via the FCC's
   Universal Licensing System (ULS) on your behalf. Processing typically takes
   a few days to two weeks. You will receive an email when your call sign is
   active in ULS.

There is no exam fee charged by the VEs (some sessions ask for a small donation
to the club). The FCC application fee is currently **$35** per class
([47 CFR 1.1102(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-1/subpart-G)).

## Call signs

Your call sign is assigned by the FCC and appears in ULS when your license is
granted. The format encodes your location (the second character is a "section"
designator) and class:

- **Technician:** `K`/`N`/`W` + 4 characters, e.g., `KB1ABC`
- **General:** same format, but the FCC may assign a shorter 3-character call
  if available
- **Amateur Extra:** eligible for a **vanity** (choice) call sign and for
  2-character "W" calls (e.g., `W1ABC`)

You may apply for a vanity call sign through ULS at any time; there is a small
fee ($60 for a new vanity, $35 to modify an existing one). Vanity calls are
assigned on a first-come basis from available combinations.

## License term and renewal

- A license is **granted for the life of the holder**. There is no expiration
  in the ordinary sense.
- However, the FCC requires a **renewal every ten years** during a specific
  window (the "Vanity Call Sign" cycle applies to vanity calls; the standard
  renewal window is announced by the FCC). During the renewal window you must
  file a renewal application in ULS and pay the fee. If you miss the window,
  the license enters a **grace period** (2 years) during which you may still
  operate but cannot renew until the next cycle; after that the call sign is
  forfeited.
- In practice: set a calendar reminder for your renewal window. It takes five
  minutes in ULS.

!!! tip "Household practice"
    For a family, one Technician license per adult who will operate is the
    minimum. The exam is genuinely passable with a weekend of study from the
    question pool — it tests familiarity with regulations and basic theory,
    not engineering. If you want HF (long-distance) capability, General is the
    practical target; the additional 35 questions cover propagation, antenna
    theory, and circuit basics at an introductory level.

## Operating under another's license

A licensee may permit another person to operate a station under their call sign
(§97.103). This is how clubs share equipment and how a parent supervises a
minor. The licensee remains responsible for the operation. A "control link"
arrangement (remote control of a repeater or remote base) has specific rules in
[§97.207–97.213](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-97.207); see
[Operating Procedure](50-operations.md#repeater-operations) if you plan to use one.
